01 · Design

Twelve shells, one rule

Every limited shell must be situationally better than the HE Shell and never flatly better. A weapon that is just "HE but more" isn't a decision, it's a resource drain — and the player learns to hoard it instead of using it.

Each shell below is defined by where it beats HE and where it's a wasted turn. If you can't write both sentences, the shell isn't designed yet. Every trajectory on the next page is computed with the tuned constants, so the diagrams show real behaviour rather than an artist's idea of it.

Four tiers

TierCountRoleAvailability
Core1The ranging instrument and the fallback. Its range table is the one players memorise.Unlimited, always equipped
Standard5Answers to a specific board state: cover, open ground, a slope, a dug-in target.Loadout, cheap
Exotic5Change the geometry of a turn rather than the damage. High cost, low ammo.Loadout, expensive
Utility1No damage. Spends a turn to change your own position in the match.Loadout
Why HE stays unlimited

There must never be a game state where a player cannot act. Unlimited HE guarantees it, and it also gives every other shell a fixed reference point — "this one arcs shorter than HE at the same power" is a sentence players can hold in their head. Take that away and the whole roster becomes twelve unrelated things to memorise.

Full stat table

Mass is the stat that surprises everyone

Mass multiplies gravity, and range goes as v² / (GRAVITY × mass). A player who has memorised "power 75 reaches the enemy" and switches to a mass-1.55 shell falls hundreds of pixels short. It's the most common mistake in the genre and it's worth showing rather than explaining:

Identical inputs — 45°, power 76, no wind. The only difference is mass. Heavy Mortar lands 316 px short of HE.

03 · Interface

Loadout & HUD

Twelve shells will not fit in the control deck, and they shouldn't. The player picks a small loadout before the match, and the HUD only ever shows what they brought.

Ten points, at most five slots, HE always free and always equipped. This is where the interesting decision lives — not in the match, where you're just spending what you already chose.

Icon set

Twelve icons on one 24×24 grid, stroke-only, drawn in currentColor so each inherits its shell's accent. They have to survive being rendered at 16 px on a chip, which is what drives every decision below.

RuleReason
Stroke-only, no fills, 1.75 px weightFills turn to mud at 16 px. Uniform weight keeps the set visually level so no icon shouts.
A ground line at y 19.5 on eight of twelveTerrain interaction is the shared semantic. The four without it — HE, Mortar, Roller, Repair — are the four that don't reshape ground.
HE and Mortar share a silhouetteMortar is deliberately read as "HE, heavier". The only differences are a fatter body and two descending chevrons for mass.
Cluster and MIRV share a fork motifSame family, different stage count — one fork versus two. A player who learns one gets the other free.
currentColor, never hardcoded fillsThe chip already colours by selection state. One icon file works selected, unselected, and greyed out.
Every path fits within 0.8–23.2A 1.75 px stroke centred on the path edge needs ~0.9 px of padding or it clips. Verified programmatically, not by eye.

Rules for the deck

RuleReason
Six slots maximum, fixed positions all matchKeys 1–6 have to mean the same thing on turn 20 as on turn 1. Muscle memory is the point.
Spent shells grey out but keep their slotRemoving a chip reflows the row and breaks every key binding mid-match.
Selecting a spent shell falls back to HENo dead selection state, ever. Slot 1 is always live.
Ammo count is always visible, never on hoverDeciding whether to spend the last mortar is the decision. Hiding the count hides the game.
Mass shown on the chip when it isn't 1.00Cheapest possible fix for the range surprise. A small ×1.55 under the name saves a wasted turn.
Loadout is where the meta lives

Ten points across eleven shells is a real deckbuilding decision, and it's the cheapest replayability you will ever add — no new code paths, just a number on each shell. It also solves the roster problem: twelve shells would be overwhelming in one deck, but picking five of eleven before a match is a pleasure.

Ship the loadout screen in the same phase as the fifth shell, not later. Six or more shells in one deck without a loadout step is where it starts feeling cluttered.

04 · Implementation

The hook model

Twelve shells, four hooks, no subclasses. Adding a thirteenth should be a table entry, not a file.

interface Shell {
  id: string; name: string; tier: Tier;
  ammo: number | 'inf';
  cost: number;              // loadout points

  // flight
  mass: number;              // × gravity — the range-changing stat
  drag: number;              // × wind susceptibility

  // detonation
  blastRadius: number;
  damage: number;
  terrain: 'carve' | 'fill' | 'column' | 'scorch' | 'none';

  // the four hooks — all optional
  onApex?:        (p, world) => void;      // Cluster, MIRV
  onAltitude?:    { alt: number, fn };      // Airburst
  onTerrainHit?:  (p, world) => Handled;   // Buster, Roller, Skipper, Drill
  onDetonate?:    (x, y, world) => void;   // Napalm, Sandbags
}

What each hook has to guarantee

HookUsed byMust terminate because
onApexCluster, MIRVChildren inherit apexDone: true. MIRV's second split is a separate counter, capped at depth 2. Without the cap a bug turns one shell into an exponential fork bomb.
onAltitudeAirburstThe fuse must arm only after the shell has exceeded the trigger altitude once. Otherwise it detonates at the muzzle, because the muzzle is already close to the ground.
onTerrainHitBuster, Roller, Skipper, DrillEvery one needs an independent hard stop: burrow distance, roll fuse, bounce count, drill depth. Never rely on the geometry alone to end the behaviour.
onDetonateNapalm, SandbagsRuns once, after damage and terrain. Fire zones need a turn counter that decrements in HANDOFF, not in FLIGHT.
The bug that will cost you an afternoon

Post-bounce and post-split projectiles spawn on or inside terrain, so the very next collision check fires immediately and the behaviour collapses into a single point. Every hook that repositions a projectile needs a short collision grace — about 14 substeps — before terrain checks resume.

This happened while building the Skipper diagram on this page: without grace, all three skips landed within 20 px of each other and it looked like the physics were broken.

Ricochet: the finding worth keeping

The Skipper was first built with real reflection about the surface normal. It was chaotic and unaimable — on the same terrain, small changes in launch angle produced wildly different outcomes, and some skips travelled backwards off a downslope:

Identical launch settings, identical terrain, both implementations run side by side. Each row lists where the shell touched down on each of its four contacts:

LaunchPhysical reflectionScripted skip
28° / 62379, 493, 449, 461379, 715, 866, 1009
30° / 62485, 494, 489, 464485, 808, 840, 907
32° / 62494, 449, 461, 462494, 812, 838, 902

The physical column is non-monotonic: on every row the shell stalls after the first contact and then travels backwards. It also has no useful relationship to the launch settings — 28° and 32° first land 115 px apart but finish within 1 px of each other, which means the player's aim stops mattering the moment the shell touches down.

The scripted version ignores the surface normal entirely: keep the direction of travel, shed 18% of horizontal speed, relaunch at a fixed shallow angle. Not physical, monotonic, completely aimable, and it reads as a skipping stone.

General principle

In an aiming game, a behaviour the player cannot predict is a behaviour they will not use. Where physical accuracy and predictability conflict, take predictability — and be explicit in the code that you did, so nobody "fixes" it back to physical six months later.

05 · Verification

Balance & tests

Damage per point, the numbers that need watching, and the assertions that catch the whole class of bugs this roster introduces.

Damage per loadout point

Theoretical maximum damage if every shell of that type connects perfectly, divided by its point cost. It is a sanity check, not a target — a shell at the bottom of this table is fine if it does something damage can't measure.

Read this table carefully

Sandbags and Repair Kit score zero and should. Their value is tempo and survival, which this metric cannot see. The real signal is any damage shell sitting far above or below the pack — that one needs its cost moved, and MIRV at four points is the one to watch, because nine submunitions across a wide spread is very swingy: near-worthless against a dug-in target, devastating on open ground.

Numbers to watch in playtest

SignalMeansFix
A shell is never pickedIts situation doesn't arise often enough, or costs too muchLower the cost first. Only buff the shell if cost 1 still doesn't move it.
A shell is always pickedIt isn't situational, it's just goodRaise cost. If it's still auto-included at 4, it violates the design rule and needs a real drawback.
Shells are hoarded to the last turnSpending feels punishingRaise ammo counts rather than damage. Scarcity should be interesting, not stressful.
Matches end before turn 8Damage is too high across the rosterScale all damage down together. Never nerf individual shells for a global problem.

Test checklist

Flight and hooks

  • Every shell's max range at power 100 on every world exceeds that world's spawn gap, or its mass is overridden for that world.
  • Cluster splits exactly once; submunitions never re-split.
  • MIRV splits exactly twice; total submunitions is exactly 9, never more.
  • Airburst fired at minimum power and elevation does not detonate at the muzzle.
  • Skipper bounces exactly three times, and every skip lands further from the firer than the last.
  • Bunker Buster burrow terminates within its stated distance or at a map boundary.
  • Drill Charge stops at the map floor rather than tunnelling past it.
  • Roller terminates on all of: fuse expiry, climb limit, map edge, hull contact.
  • Every projectile repositioned by a hook gets a collision grace period before terrain checks resume.

Damage and terrain

  • Self-damage applies at full value for every shell, including submunitions.
  • Sandbags never sets a solid pixel inside any hull box, including the firer's.
  • Napalm fire zones decrement in HANDOFF exactly once per round, not once per turn per player.
  • Napalm damage stops when the zone expires even if the tank is still standing in it.
  • Repair Kit cannot be used on two consecutive turns by the same player.
  • Repair Kit never takes a tank above 100 health.
  • Drill Charge's vertical column carve does not read or write outside the terrain mask at map edges.

Loadout and UI

  • A loadout can never exceed 10 points or 5 slots.
  • HE occupies slot 1 in every loadout and cannot be removed.
  • Keys 1–6 map to the same shell for the entire match, including after shells are spent.
  • Selecting a spent shell falls back to HE rather than leaving a dead selection.
  • Chips show mass when it differs from 1.00.
  • Both players' loadouts persist across rounds within a match; ammo resets each round.
