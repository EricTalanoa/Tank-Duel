01 · The premise

Different planets, honestly costed

The twist is good and it works, but not the way it first sounds. Real planetary gravity applied literally destroys the game, and it's worth seeing exactly how before designing around it.

What literal gravity does

Take the tuned Earth constants and swap in real surface gravity ratios. Range at power 100, 45°, flat ground:

Bodyg vs EarthRange @100Flight timeVerdict
Jupiter2.53475 px0.7sPlayable, but the map must shrink to ~400 px
Venus0.9051324 px1.9sPlayable as-is
Mars0.3793145 px4.6sNeeds a 2600 px map and a camera
Mercury0.3783157 px4.6sSame as Mars — indistinguishable in play
Io0.1836505 px9.6sTen seconds staring at a dot
The Moon0.1657218 px10.6sUnplayable
Europa0.1348893 px13.1sUnplayable
Ceres0.02941057 px60.5sOne minute per shot
The constraint is flight time, not range

Range is fixable — make the map wider and add a camera. Flight time is not, because it's dead air the player watches. Terra sits at 1.75s, which playtests as about right. The tolerable band is roughly 1.0s to 2.2s. Anything past three seconds and players start talking over the shot instead of watching it.

Since T = 2·v·sin(θ) / g, halving gravity doubles flight time. That single relationship is what caps the design.

The fix: three dials, not one

Rather than picking gravity and accepting the consequences, each world sets three values together so that flight time lands back in the playable band:

Gravity multiplier
The tactical dial. Changes arc shape, and invalidates every power setting the player has memorised. This is the actual gameplay content.
Map width
Scales roughly with 1/g so that power 100 slightly overshoots the far edge. Keeps the power slider fully useful on every world.
Flight time scale
The sim steps faster or slower during FLIGHT only. Low-gravity worlds run at 1.6–3× so a 5.8s arc is watched in under 2s.
Atmosphere (bonus)
Wind range and true air drag. This is what makes two worlds with similar gravity feel completely different.

Time scaling is cheap and safe: run N sim steps per rendered frame instead of one. It's an integer count, so determinism survives intact and replays still work.

Why atmosphere earns its place

Gravity alone gives you one axis, and after three worlds it's just "the numbers are bigger". Air drag is a genuinely different feel: on a thick-atmosphere world shells decelerate, so range collapses, arcs go asymmetric — steep on the way down — and wind dominates gravity. Vesper below has almost Earth gravity and plays nothing like Terra.

Side by side

Balance consequence you have to accept

Changing gravity changes every weapon's effective range, and the mass multiplier compounds it. The Heavy Mortar at mass 1.55 on Ferrum at g 1.75 has an effective gravity of 2.71× — its max range drops to about 250 px. On a 580 px map that means it cannot cross from spawn.

Fix it in one place: on world load, assert that every weapon's max range exceeds the spawn gap, and auto-drop mass for any weapon that fails. That check would have caught the original mortar bug too.

03 · Shape

Terrain generators

Physics is one axis, shape is the other. Six generators × six worlds is 36 combinations from about 90 lines of code, and they're independently selectable so a player can put canyon terrain on a low-gravity world.

Why this multiplies rather than adds

A generator changes what the tactics are; a world changes what the arithmetic is. Canyon on Terra is a familiar lob into a pit. Canyon on Ferrum is a knife fight where the pit wall is unclimbable and the Bunker Buster becomes the only real weapon. Neither needed new code.

GeneratorFavoursPunishesWatch for
HillsNothing in particular — the neutral baselineNothingOccasionally generates a blocking central peak; validation must reject it
CanyonHigh-arc shots, Airburst, Bunker BusterFlat trajectories, RollerSpawns must be on the rims, never in the pit
CratersRoller (every crater is a slope), splash weaponsDirect fire — targets sit below the rimOverlapping craters can isolate a tank on a pillar
PlatesPrecise ranging — flat tops mean consistent impactsRoller, which stops dead on every terraceVertical faces need the roller climb limit tested hard
SpiresCluster and MIRV, which fall between the columnsEverything flat — most shots hit a spireThin columns are the tunnelling worst case; test at power 100
RingWrapping shots, long-way-round attacksNothing — but it changes how aiming reads entirelySeam continuity: h[0] must equal h[W-1] or shells jump
Ring is the one worth building first

Wrapping is a small change — modulo the x coordinate in collision, rendering, and the camera — and it produces the most distinctive play in the set. On Hollow a power-100 shot laps the world three times, so you can hit someone by aiming away from them, and the correct answer to "they're dug in on my left" is sometimes to shoot right.

Constraint: the generator must be seamless. Building it from sinusoids with integer periods over the map width guarantees it — the version generated on this page closes to within 0.5 px.

04 · Interface

Map select screen

The screen sits between mode select and the round intro. Its job is to make the physics difference legible before the player commits, because a world that silently invalidates your power settings feels broken rather than fresh.

What the card has to communicate

ElementCarriesWhy
Terrain silhouetteGenerator shape and world paletteRecognition. After two matches players pick by silhouette, not name.
Gravity figureThe single number that changes their aimIt's the only stat that invalidates muscle memory. Lead with it.
Atmosphere tagVacuum / thin / thickPredicts whether wind will matter more than gravity.
Map width barRelative engagement distanceCheap to draw, instantly explains why power settings won't carry over.
The thing that will annoy players

Switching worlds resets the range table they've internalised, which is the point — but it feels like the game broke unless you tell them. On the first round of any world other than Terra, show the ranging hint under the power readout: "Power 75 travels ~1490 px here", using the world's own computed figure. One line, first round only, and it converts a moment of confusion into a moment of understanding.

Selection rules

  • Terra is the default and the only world offered in a first-time player's first match.
  • World and terrain generator are chosen independently — never bundle them, or you lose the 36 combinations for no reason.
  • Random is a first-class option and should be the most-used one. Put it in the grid, not in a menu.
  • In best-of-3 and best-of-5, offer rotate worlds each round. It's the best argument for the whole feature: the player who adapts fastest wins the match.

05 · Implementation

What this costs to build

Most of it is parameterisation you should do anyway. Two items are real work, and one of them is optional.

Change list

ChangeWhereSizeNotes
Constants become per-worldsim/world.tsSmallGRAVITY, WIND_K, wind range, air drag, map width move from module constants onto the world object. Do this even if you ship only Terra.
Air drag termsim/ballistics.tsSmallOne line: vx *= (1-drag); vy *= (1-drag) per frame, after integration. Zero on most worlds.
Variable map widthterrain, render, cameraMediumEvery hardcoded 1000 becomes world.width. Grep for it now, before there are more.
Scrolling camerarender/camera.tsLargeRequired for anything wider than ~1300 px. Follows the shell in flight, frames both tanks in AIM. This is the real cost of the feature.
Flight time scalemain loopSmallsteps = world.timeScale extra sim steps per frame during FLIGHT only. Integer, so determinism holds.
Horizontal wrapterrain, ballistics, renderMediumModulo x in collision, carve, and draw. Optional — only Hollow needs it.
Generator registrysim/terrain/gen/SmallSix functions with one signature: (seed, width, height) => Float32Array.
Per-world validationworld loadSmallAssert every weapon can cross the spawn gap. Catches the mortar class of bug automatically.
Sequencing advice

The camera is the gate. Until it exists you can only ship worlds at or below about 1300 px wide, which is Terra, Vesper and Ferrum — and those three already give you a full-gravity spread from 0.90 to 1.75 plus a thick-atmosphere world that plays completely differently.

Ship those three in phase 7 with no camera work at all. Build the camera afterwards, and Rust, Selene and Hollow become almost free.

World definition shape

Everything on this page comes from objects like this. Adding a seventh world should be data, not code.

{
  id:        'vesper',
  name:      'Vesper',
  gravity:   0.90,     // × base 0.215
  airDrag:   0.012,    // velocity damping per frame, 0 = vacuum
  width:     620,
  windRange: 60,
  windMode:  'fixed',  // 'reroll' | 'fixed' | 'none'
  timeScale: 1,
  wrap:      false,
  gen:       'spires',
  palette:   { sky:['#2A1420','#5C2438','#C46A52'], scrub:'#6B4238',
               dirt:'#5A3128', rock:'#331C18' }
}

Test checklist

  • Every world's power-100 range at 45° exceeds its map width.
  • Every weapon's max range on every world exceeds that world's spawn gap.
  • Perceived flight time after time-scaling is between 1.0s and 2.2s on all worlds.
  • Ring generator seam: |h[0] − h[width−1]| < 1 px.
  • On a wrapping world, a shell crossing the seam has a continuous trail and carves across the boundary correctly.
  • Time scaling changes only the step count, never the step size — identical inputs produce identical terrain at any scale.
  • No hardcoded 1000 or 560 remains outside the world definitions.
  • Switching worlds between rounds resets terrain, health and ammo, but not the player's chosen loadout.
