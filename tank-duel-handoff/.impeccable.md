## Design Context

### Users

Local hotseat players sharing one screen. They need to enter a match quickly, understand the bracketing mechanic, and replay without navigating unnecessary setup.

### Brand Personality

Tactical, mechanical, dramatic. The interface should feel like a physical artillery range instrument with personality, not a generic sci-fi dashboard or playful toy.

### Aesthetic Direction

Dark, restrained field-at-dusk presentation using the established artillery palette and shell icon language. The title canvas supplies the memorable hero moment through silhouettes, atmosphere, and exchanged fire. Menus remain crisp, legible, and subordinate to that scene. Avoid neon cyberpunk, glassmorphism, generic card grids, gradient text, bounce, and elastic motion.

### Design Principles

- Make the two-click route into local play unmistakable.
- Treat trails, ballistics, and bracketing as information; decorative motion never obscures them.
- Use one orchestrated title scene instead of animating every control.
- Keep canvas work bounded for stable 60 fps and provide a materially quieter reduced-motion alternative.
- Preserve semantic controls, visible focus, spec-owned copy, and shell icons on every named-shell surface.
